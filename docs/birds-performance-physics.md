## Result

Preserved the original `EnemyBird16-strip.png` byte for byte (Git blob `d069289580f4dbe6cd2b895c2acb9ed10cd38293`), original color treatments, anchors, timing, interpolation and patrols. Filtered strips are flattened once, and the finite 168 interpolated frames are decoded to small PNG Images in batches of four. Critical fonts load first. Small frames use immediate PNG encoding because Chromium can postpone asynchronous `toBlob` work behind a continuously animating page; the three larger source strips use asynchronous encoding. No sprite filtering, compositing, encoding or decoding occurs in the steady render loop. Temporary blob URLs are revoked and construction canvases released. Reduced motion prepares only three gliding textures. Original sprites remain visible during preparation or an encoding failure; the existing visible fallback remains for source failure.

Connected strokes use a canonical drawing direction and at most half a world unit of collision-only simplification. Visible paths and ink accounting are preserved. The solver resolves the nearest connected feature, uses radial impulses that preserve the orthogonal tangent at rounded joints, suppresses low-speed micro-bounces, and applies rolling drag once per substep based on elapsed time. Sleeping requires flat support or opposing contacts, so genuine flat shelves/valleys can settle while gentle downhill slopes keep rolling. Floor, release, collision substeps, speed limits, results, rewards, persistence and level definitions remain intact.

Final local update/draw measurements (96 samples per case): desktop Chromium mean 0.33 ms / p95 0.60 ms; Android viewport Chromium 0.22 / 0.40; phone Windows WebKit 0.46 / 1; tablet Windows WebKit 0.41 / 1. Compare the isolated tablet baseline of 60.5 / 236. This measures engine work on this Windows host, not physical-device FPS or compositor/thermal behavior. Shared large image sheets were rejected after increasing one mobile Chromium case to 28.7 ms; final rendering uses small images.

### Result and cause

The baseline sweep reproduced 45 non-floor stalls out of 270 drops: all 15 tested claw positions in levels 8, 15 and 16 eventually reached rails ending too close to the right wall. The 13-unit opening could not pass a 24-unit-diameter stone. Those three downhill ends now leave a usable opening; other stage geometry, original birds, patrols and guide solutions remain intact. Baseline: `output/birds-rolling/baseline.json` and `baseline-desktop-details.json`.

The collision solver still uses the existing connected-stroke simplification, radial impulses and elapsed-time drag. Gameplay now adds a modest tangent nudge after 0.65 seconds without two units of movement, so flat/nearly flat drawn runways roll toward an exit. Floor resting gets no assistance. A separate 2.6-second movement window detects genuinely trapped routes; as explicitly selected by the user, an unfinished trapped run returns to the claw with lines, ink, aim and hint penalty retained. Partial bird progress resets for the retry, and recovery awards no points or unlocks. Completed runs retain their win/result and award once. This is deliberate recovery for enclosed paths, not collision phasing through a rail or a claim that an enclosed shape has a physical exit.

Only the floor can put an unfinished stone to sleep. The collider array is rebuilt when geometry changes rather than allocated at every collision substep. The visible ball and floor shadow interpolate between fixed 60-Hz physics poses. Release, pause/resume, claw return and rest snap their rendering history appropriately; a resting stone cannot replay a stale movement segment. Physics positions and collision timing remain independent of display refresh.
